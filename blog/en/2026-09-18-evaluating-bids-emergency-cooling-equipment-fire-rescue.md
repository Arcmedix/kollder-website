Once bids are in, the most legally exposed step is the evaluation itself: a scoring matrix applied inconsistently, or claims left unverified, can ground a legal challenge. This guide walks through how to evaluate bids for emergency cooling equipment objectively.

**Short answer:** evaluating bids for emergency cooling equipment requires applying the published scoring matrix strictly, verifying technical claims through a timed demonstration and reference calls, and documenting every score in the evaluation report.

To write the specification upstream, see our [clause-by-clause guide](https://kollder.com/blog/en/2026-09-18-writing-technical-specification-emergency-cooling-fire-rescue/). This document focuses on evaluating the bids received.

## Apply the published scoring matrix strictly

The rule is simple: the scoring matrix published in the tender documents must be applied exactly as stated during evaluation, with no sub-criteria added or dropped after the fact. A common weighting for this type of equipment:

- 40 to 50% technical merit (compliance with whole-body immersion protocol, demonstrated deployment time, patient access, material durability)
- 30 to 40% price, assessed on total cost of ownership (purchase, spare parts, warranty) rather than purchase price alone
- 10 to 20% references and after-sales support

## Verify technical claims, don't just read them

A technical submission states performance figures; evaluation should verify them. Three practical checks:

- **Timed demonstration**: require a real deployment on unprepared ground similar to your operational conditions, and confirm the structure stays stable and level across all support points. A gap between the stated and measured time is a major warning sign.
- **Reference verification**: contact the agencies or services cited as references directly to confirm real-world use and satisfaction, beyond the list supplied by the bidder.
- **Documentation consistency check**: compare the dimensions, weight, and materials stated in the technical submission against the manufacturer's public product documentation. Any inconsistency should be questioned before scoring.

## Warning signs during evaluation

- A price significantly below other bids should be questioned as a potential abnormally low bid
- A spec sheet that does not state exact materials or a measured deployment time is incomplete and should be flagged for clarification where the procedure allows it
- A bidder who declines a field demonstration before award is a signal worth weighing in your risk assessment

## Document the evaluation report

The bid evaluation report should show, criterion by criterion, how each bid was scored and why. An unjustified score exposes the process to a transparency challenge. Keep records of verifications carried out (demonstration, reference calls) as an appendix.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Implicitly changing the published weighting during evaluation
- Scoring based on the written submission alone, without a field demonstration
- Ignoring an abnormally low price without questioning it
- Failing to document the verifications carried out, leaving the report open to challenge
