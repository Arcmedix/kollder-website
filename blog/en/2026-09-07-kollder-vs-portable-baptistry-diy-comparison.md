Churches equipping themselves for immersion baptism today generally weigh three options: the Kollder portable baptistry, the US-based Portable Baptistry, or a makeshift solution such as an inflatable pool. Here's an objective comparison based on each option's publicly available specifications.

## Overview of the options

**Portable Baptistry** is a well-established US baptistry brand, built on a modular sectional structure (tank plus cabinet), available in several sizes and sometimes with a wood cabinet finish (unfinished oak or birch). According to the manufacturer, larger models accommodate up to 3 people standing and set up in minutes by one person.

**Makeshift solutions** (inflatable PVC pools, home bathtubs) remain the most common option in the absence of a known alternative, particularly outside the US where dedicated baptistry suppliers are scarce.

**Kollder** is a rigid folding baptistry in food-grade stainless steel, derived from Kollder One™ equipment, built for setup in under 2 minutes by one person.

| Criterion | Kollder | Portable Baptistry | Makeshift (inflatable) |

## What Portable Baptistry does well

**An established, proven offering.** The US portable baptistry market is more mature than in most other countries, with a product track record that reassures buyers. **Generous capacity** on the larger models, accommodating up to 3 people standing according to the manufacturer, a relevant format for group baptisms. **A wood cabinet finish** available on some models, which gives a more furniture-like, permanent presence for a lasting indoor installation.

## What makeshift solutions offer

The main advantage remains immediate availability and a very low entry cost, a real argument for a church testing immersion practice for the first time without committing a dedicated budget.

## What sets Kollder apart

**Folded compactness.** At 85 × 10 × 10 cm (33 × 4 × 4 in) and 11 kg (24 lb), Kollder's folded format is significantly more compact than a stackable sectional structure, an advantage for storage and transport between locations.

**The material in contact with water.** Food-grade stainless steel cleans and disinfects with no degradation over time. A wood cabinet, even sealed, requires more attentive upkeep under repeated exposure to moisture.

**European logistics.** Manufactured and available without the lead time and cost of transatlantic shipping for a product made in the US, a concrete point for a church based in Europe.

## Conclusion

The right choice mostly depends on the church's profile. For a one-off test on a minimal budget, a makeshift solution remains a reasonable short-term option despite its faster wear. For substantial group capacity and a permanent cabinet presence, Portable Baptistry has the advantage of its track record in the US market. For a church that prioritizes fast single-person setup, an easy-to-maintain material built for years of use, and logistics suited to Europe, the Kollder portable baptistry addresses those priorities directly.
