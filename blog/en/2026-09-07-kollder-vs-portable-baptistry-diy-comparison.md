Churches equipping themselves for immersion baptism today generally weigh three options: the Kollder portable baptistry, the US-based Portable Baptistry, or a makeshift solution such as an inflatable pool. Here's an objective comparison based on each option's publicly available specifications and on the two criteria that matter most during the ceremony: **how stable the tank is** and **how much room there is for the candidate and the officiant**.

## The options at a glance

**Kollder** is a rigid folding baptistry: a stainless steel frame, SS304 stainless hinges, **6 support points and cross-bracing**. The **205 × 80 × 60 cm (81 × 31 × 24 in)** tank holds **up to two people at once**, the candidate lying down and the officiant beside them in the water, and stays level even on uneven ground. Folded, it fits in an 85 × 10 × 10 cm (33 × 4 × 4 in) bag.

**Portable Baptistry** is a well-established US baptistry brand, built on a modular sectional structure (tank plus cabinet), available in several sizes and sometimes with a wood cabinet finish (unfinished oak or birch). According to the manufacturer, larger models accommodate up to 3 people standing.

**Makeshift solutions** (inflatable PVC pool, domestic bathtub) remain common for lack of a known alternative, especially in markets where dedicated baptistries are hard to find.

## What Portable Baptistry does well

**An established, proven offering.** The US portable baptistry market is more mature than in most other countries, with a product track record that reassures buyers. **Generous capacity** on the larger models, accommodating up to 3 people standing according to the manufacturer, a relevant format for group baptisms. **A wood cabinet finish** available on some models, which gives a more furniture-like, permanent presence for a lasting indoor installation.

## What makeshift solutions offer

The main advantage remains immediate availability and a very low entry cost, a real argument for a church testing immersion practice for the first time without committing a dedicated budget. Their limit shows as soon as two adults step into the water: an inflatable wall gives way under a hand, the floor sags, and the moment loses both safety and dignity.

## What sets Kollder apart

**Structural stability.** The tank doesn't rely on air pressure or on assembled sections: the steel frame, its 6 support points and its cross-bracing keep it level on an old wooden floor, tiles, a lawn or a campground, even with several hundred litres of water and two adults moving inside.

**Room for the candidate and the officiant together.** At 205 cm (81 in) long, the tank lets the candidate lie at full length while the officiant supports them in the water, so the whole gesture stays controlled from start to finish. That makes the difference for an elderly candidate, someone anxious about water, or a person with reduced mobility. The details are in [Portable Baptistry: Room for Two People and Full Immersion](/blog/en/2026-09-08-portable-baptistry-two-people-full-immersion/).

**Folded compactness.** At 85 × 10 × 10 cm (33 × 4 × 4 in) and about 11 kg (24 lb), Kollder's folded format is significantly more compact than a stackable sectional structure, an advantage for storage in a closet or vestry and for transport between locations.

**The material in contact with water.** Stainless steel cleans and disinfects with no degradation over time. A wood cabinet, even sealed, requires more attentive upkeep under repeated exposure to moisture.

## Conclusion

The right choice mostly depends on the church's profile. For a one-off test on a minimal budget, a makeshift solution remains a reasonable short-term option despite its faster wear and limited stability. For substantial group capacity and a permanent cabinet presence, Portable Baptistry has the advantage of its track record in the US market. For a church that wants a tank that doesn't move, room for the candidate and the officiant together, and a baptistry that packs into a bag between ceremonies, the Kollder portable baptistry addresses those priorities directly.
