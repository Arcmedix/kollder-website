Unlike a medical cooling tub, a baptistry needs comfortable water. Someone standing still in the water for a few minutes, lightly clothed, feels the cold quickly. Here is the range to aim for and the practical ways to reach it.

## The right temperature: 82 to 95°F (28 to 35°C)

- Water around **86°F (30°C)** is the best compromise: warm enough to stand still without shivering, without being uncomfortably tepid.
- Below **77°F (25°C)**, discomfort sets in fast, especially for an elderly person or a child.
- There's no need to aim for body temperature (98.6°F / 37°C): the immersion is brief, and water that hot is unpleasant on entry.

## How much water you need to heat

For a 205 × 80 × 60 cm (81 × 31 × 24 in) pool, plan for **130 to 185 gallons**. Raising 600 liters by 18°F (10°C) takes roughly **7 kWh** of energy, which is why a supplemental heater has to be started several hours ahead.

## Method 1: mixing in hot water

The simplest approach, with no special equipment.

- Fill part of the pool with cold water from the hose, then top up with hot water from the building's supply (a mixer tap, buckets).
- Rule of thumb: to bring cold tap water (around 59°F / 15°C) up to about 86°F (30°C), use roughly **1 part hot water (140°F / 60°C) to 2 parts cold**.
- Stir the water and check the temperature in several spots before the service.

## Method 2: a supplemental heater or immersion heater

- A submersible pool or spa heater can bring the water up to temperature, but plan for **several hours** and a dedicated power socket.
- Electrical safety is non-negotiable: the unit must be protected by a GFCI/RCD, **never energized while anyone is in the water**, removed and unplugged before the service begins, with no extension leads near the water.

## Method 3: fill ahead from the building's supply

- Fill the pool the evening before from a mixer tap set to the right temperature.
- A cover or blanket over the pool limits heat loss overnight.

## What makes the water lose heat

- The large water surface, a cold floor, draughts. Water heated too early can drop several degrees before the service.
- Fill as close to the scheduled time as possible, cover the pool while it waits, and avoid setting the baptistry up under an air vent.
