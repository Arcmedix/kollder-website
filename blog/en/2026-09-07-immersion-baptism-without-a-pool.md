Many Baptist, evangelical, and Pentecostal churches practice believer's baptism by full immersion, but meet in places with no built-in pool: a rented hall, a former retail space, a tent. The same question comes up before every baptism service: where and how do you immerse the candidate properly?

## The usual workarounds, and their limits

Three options come up most often in congregations without a permanent baptistry.

**The municipal pool.** Some local pools allow a booking outside regular hours. It works, but the slot is rarely flexible, the setting isn't conducive to a meaningful moment, and you have to request it again for every service.

**A lake, river, or the ocean.** Historically the closest option to the biblical practice. Still viable for a church near a safe, accessible body of water, but it depends entirely on weather, season, and site safety.

**An inflatable pool or a bathtub.** A common stopgap, but an inflatable pool takes time to inflate and fill, punctures easily, and a home bathtub is rarely large enough for full immersion of an adult.

## What a portable baptistry changes

A **portable baptistry** directly solves these three constraints. It sets up indoors or outdoors, right in the sanctuary if needed, with no dependence on an outside booking or a natural water source. Filled with a garden hose or water containers, it offers enough room for full adult immersion while staying compact enough to store between services.

## Planning ahead for the day

Whatever solution a church chooses, a few things are worth planning in advance: water temperature (it can be warmed ahead of time, with none of the cold-water requirement of a medical tub), how many people will be in the water with the candidate, and access for the officiant from both sides of the pool.
