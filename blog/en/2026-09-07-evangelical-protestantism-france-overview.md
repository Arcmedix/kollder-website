Evangelical Protestantism holds a growing place in the French religious landscape, one that's often little understood outside the communities themselves. Here's a data-based overview, drawn from figures published by the [National Council of French Evangelicals (CNEF)](https://www.lecnef.org/page/170867-le-cnef).

## How many evangelicals in France

According to figures published by the CNEF, France today counts **2,530 evangelical places of worship** and roughly **745,000 practicing believers**. The CNEF alone represents **more than 70% of France's evangelical Protestant churches**, bringing together 36 church unions and 173 member associations nationwide.

On the wider Protestant side, the [Protestant Federation of France (FPF)](https://www.protestants.org/trouver-une-eglise-protestante-pres-de-chez-soi/) federates 26 church unions, 7 associated churches, and 12 communities, plus more than 70 associations, representing close to 500 communities, institutions, and Protestant movements across the country.

## Organized around church unions

The French evangelical landscape isn't a single bloc but a collection of church unions with varying traditions: Baptist, Pentecostal, evangelical Reformed, charismatic, and communities rooted in African or Portuguese-speaking expression, among others. Each union brings together dozens to hundreds of local congregations around a shared theological outlook, while also taking part in national bodies such as the CNEF.

## Spread across the whole country

The CNEF's regional network covers the entire French territory, with churches present in major cities as well as mid-sized towns and rural areas. This wide spread partly explains the variety of settings these congregations operate in: some occupy a historic building, others rent a multipurpose hall, a former retail space, or a tent for their services.

## What that means for church equipment

This variety of situations has a direct practical consequence: a church that doesn't own its building, or that has no fixed baptistry in it, needs a workable solution to keep practicing immersion baptism, a central practice across most of these traditions. That's exactly the role a portable baptistry plays, built to adapt to any worship space rather than the other way around.
