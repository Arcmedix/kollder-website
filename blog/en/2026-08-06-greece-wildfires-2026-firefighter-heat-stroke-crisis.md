Two firefighting helicopters collided near Athens on August 2, killing a Danish pilot and a Greek fire service official. A week earlier, three firefighters died battling blazes on Crete and the mainland. Nearly 500 personnel are still fighting a fire west of Athens that has already burned more than 10,000 hectares. Greece is now in its fourth consecutive summer of wildfire crises this severe, and the exertional heat stroke risk carried by the firefighters on the ground remains the least discussed part of the story.

## A fire season stacking on top of Europe's worst wind conditions in years

According to the European Space Agency's Copernicus program, a major wildfire that broke out on July 31 near Psatha and Porto Germeno, west of Athens, had burned more than 10,000 hectares by August 2, destroying or damaging over 100 homes. Greece's Civil Protection service issued a red alert, its highest category, for the region as gale-force winds reaching 9 on the Beaufort scale, with gusts up to 11, drove the fire across a fourth consecutive day. On the same weekend, separate blazes were active in Fokida, Megara and on the island of Kefalonia.

The toll from earlier in the season was already severe. On July 30, wildfires forced the evacuation of thousands of people in Crete, destroying farmland, olive groves and homes, and killing three firefighters. Wildfires were also reported on Paros. In early July, fires damaged property in Thessaloniki, and Greek authorities counted dozens of separate fires burning across the country on a single day, July 5.

## Wind, radiant heat and multi-day rotations: the physiological math firefighters are working against

What makes the 2026 Greek season distinct is not just heat, but wind speed compounding it. Gusts of 80 to 100 km/h have repeatedly grounded firefighting aircraft, according to multiple on-scene reports, forcing ground crews to hold lines without the aerial support that normally shortens exposure time. A senior Greek fire official described conditions in which "nature and the intensity of weather phenomena surpass any human planning and any operational capability," a line that captures why this year's rotations have run longer and harder than a typical Mediterranean fire season.

For the firefighters working those lines, the exertional heat stroke risk factors stack directly on top of one another. **Turnout gear** covering the majority of body surface area sharply limits evaporative cooling exactly when physical exertion is highest. **Wind-driven fire behavior** turns what should be contained operations into extended, unpredictable engagements, as seen with the Porto Germeno fire's fourth straight day of active fighting. **Radiant heat** from the flame front adds to an ambient temperature already at seasonal highs across Attica and the islands. None of this is new to Mediterranean fire services, but the frequency with which Greece is now recording multiple concurrent large fires, four consecutive summers running, means crews are absorbing this risk profile for longer stretches each year.

## Cool First, Transport Second applies on the fireground, not just after it

The **ACSM Expert Consensus Statement 2023** and the IOC's guidance (Hosokawa Y, Racinais S et al., BJSM 2021) are explicit: when exertional heat stroke is suspected, cold water immersion must begin on scene, before transport, to bring core temperature below 39°C within 30 minutes of collapse. Casa et al. (Exercise and Sport Sciences Reviews, 2007) documented a cooling rate of 0.35°C per minute for this method, roughly ten times faster than wet towels or ice packs. The Korey Stringer Institute's dataset of more than 401 cases treated under this protocol reports a 100 percent survival rate when core temperature drops below 40°C within that window.

The operational question for Hellenic Fire Service rehab sectors, and for any fire service supporting them through EU mutual aid, is whether that capability exists at the point of collapse, on a mountainside near Porto Germeno or a coastal track on Crete, rather than only once a casualty reaches a fixed medical post.
