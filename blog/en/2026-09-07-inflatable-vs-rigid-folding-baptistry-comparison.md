Once a church decides to invest in immersion baptism equipment, a practical question follows: inflatable baptistry or rigid folding structure? Both exist on the market, with very different trade-offs.

## The inflatable baptistry

**Advantages.** Lightweight, easy to store deflated, usually a lower upfront cost.

**Limits.** Inflating takes time, typically several minutes with an electric pump, which means planning ahead. The soft PVC structure is also more prone to wear: a punctured chamber or a faulty valve is enough to make the baptistry unusable on the day. On uneven flooring, stability depends entirely on air pressure, which can feel unsteady during immersion.

## The rigid folding baptistry

**Advantages.** A frame structure (typically steel or aluminum) provides constant stability, independent of air pressure. Setup happens by unfolding rather than inflating, which sharply cuts installation time. A food-grade stainless steel frame cleans and disinfects easily, for repeated use over many years with no degradation.

**Limits.** Folded weight is generally higher than an inflatable model, though a good wheeled carry bag largely offsets that in practice.

## What actually matters for a church

For occasional use (a handful of baptisms a year), the lower upfront cost may point toward an inflatable model. But for a church that practices immersion regularly, or that wants to organize a baptism without hours of setup planning, the rigid folding structure becomes the more reliable option: it **sets up in a few minutes**, with no electric pump or pressure to monitor, and its lifespan far exceeds that of an inflatable structure subject to PVC wear.
