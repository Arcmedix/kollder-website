Once RFP responses are in, the decisive step remains: telling a reliable vendor apart from an unverified sales pitch. On an event medical setup, this decision directly affects participant safety. This guide walks through how to evaluate vendors objectively.

**Short answer:** evaluating an event cooling equipment vendor requires a timed demonstration before the event, direct verification of a comparable event reference, and a comparison matrix scoring every proposal on the same criteria rather than on sales pitch alone.

To build your RFP upstream, see our [template and questions to ask vendors](https://kollder.com/blog/en/2026-09-18-rfp-template-event-cooling-equipment-vendor/). This document focuses on evaluating the responses you get back.

## Verify before you trust

Three simple checks, routinely skipped for lack of time, prevent most bad surprises:

- **Ask for a timed demonstration**, even a quick one, before the event rather than on race day. A real deployment reveals gaps a spec sheet does not show.
- **Contact a cited event reference directly**, ideally one comparable in format to yours (distance, turnout, terrain). A strong reference from a very different format is not a reliable guarantee for yours.
- **Check consistency across documents**: dimensions, deployment time, and pricing terms should match from one document to the next.

## Warning signs not to ignore

- A vendor who consistently declines or postpones a demonstration before the event
- A stated deployment time that changes depending on who you ask or which document you check
- No verifiable reference from a sports event, even a small one
- Vague insurance or liability terms for the equipment supplied in the event of an incident

## Build a simple comparison matrix

To compare vendors objectively without being swayed by sales pitch, score every proposal on the same criteria:

- Demonstrated (not stated) deployment time
- Patient access during immersion
- Portability suited to your event format
- Verified references from comparable events
- Pricing terms (rental vs. purchase) and flexibility
- Responsiveness and clarity of answers during the exchange

A vendor who answers every question precisely, with documentation you can verify, is as strong a signal as the product itself.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Deciding on sales pitch confidence alone, without field verification
- Consulting only one vendor, even if the first contact is convincing
- Accepting an event reference without checking it directly
- Deciding too late, leaving no time to arrange a demonstration before the event
