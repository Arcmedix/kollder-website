Writing an RFP for emergency cooling equipment means putting in writing, clause by clause, how a clinical requirement translates into verifiable criteria. This guide walks through the document structure, sample clauses, and a scoring matrix applicable across sectors.

**Short answer:** an RFP for emergency cooling equipment should word every requirement as a measurable performance level (deployment under 2 minutes, whole-body immersion, patient access), require a timed demonstration, and publish the scoring matrix to bidders upfront.

For the overall purchasing method (scoping, procurement route, evaluation), see our [complete guide on preparing a tender for emergency cooling equipment](https://kollder.com/blog/en/2026-09-18-how-to-prepare-tender-emergency-cooling-equipment-guide/). This document focuses on writing the RFP itself.

## Standard RFP structure

1. Context and purpose of the request
2. Functional and clinical requirements
3. Measurable technical requirements
4. Demonstration terms
5. Expected commercial and support terms
6. Scoring matrix shared with bidders

## 1. Context and purpose of the request

> "This request for proposal covers the supply of a cold water immersion cooling system for the treatment of exertional heat stroke, consistent with the 'Cool First, Transport Second' protocol (ACSM 2023, IOC/BJSM 2021)."

## 2. Functional and clinical requirements

> "The equipment must allow whole-body immersion, shoulders and scalp included, with continuous patient access throughout immersion: airway monitoring, temperature checks, and resuscitation if required."

## 3. Measurable technical requirements

> "Full deployment achievable in under 2 minutes by a single operator, on unprepared ground, verified through a timed demonstration. Structure and liner able to withstand repeated use under demanding conditions (impact, UV exposure, disinfectant products). Folded dimensions and weight compatible with [specify storage or transport context]."

Word every requirement as a measurable performance level, never as a specific product or brand.

## 4. Demonstration terms

> "Shortlisted bidders shall carry out a timed deployment demonstration prior to final award. Any discrepancy from the performance stated in the written proposal may result in disqualification."

## 5. Expected commercial and support terms

> "The bidder shall specify delivery timelines, warranty terms, spare parts availability and lead time, and user training arrangements."

## 6. Scoring matrix to share with bidders

Publishing the scoring matrix inside the RFP itself improves the quality of responses and makes the selection process defensible:

- 40 to 50% technical merit (protocol compliance, demonstrated deployment time, durability)
- 30 to 40% price, assessed on total cost of ownership
- 10 to 20% references and after-sales support

## Common drafting mistakes

- Writing unmeasurable requirements ("durable," "easy to use") without defining them
- Not sharing the scoring matrix with bidders, which weakens the quality of responses received
- Omitting the timed demonstration clause before final decision
- Copying a generic RFP without adapting it to your actual use context
