Since April 2026, Germany's Robert Koch Institute (RKI) has recorded an estimated 14,000 heat-related deaths nationwide, with a confidence range of 12,500 to 15,300. It is by far the deadliest heat year the country has recorded since heat mortality tracking began in 1992. Nearly seven in ten of those deaths occurred in a single week at the end of June, a concentration that also translated into an unprecedented operational surge for fire and emergency medical services across the country.

## A toll with no precedent since 1992

Germany's previous annual record was set in 2018, with around 9,000 heat-related deaths across the entire year. That threshold was already surpassed by early August 2026. The scale of the shift becomes clear when compared to recent summers over an equivalent reporting period: 1,600 deaths in 2023, 1,210 in 2024, and 1,620 in 2025. The 2026 heat season is therefore running at roughly eight to nine times the average of the previous three years.

An RKI spokeswoman described the 12,500 estimate as conservative, noting that the final toll could climb higher once all regional data is fully reconciled.

## The week that broke the statistics

Of the roughly 14,000 heat deaths recorded through calendar week 32 (early August), about 9,600 occurred during calendar week 26 alone, the week of 22 to 28 June. On 27 June, 46 of the country's roughly 200 weather stations exceeded 40°C. The night of 27 to 28 June was, according to current data from the Deutscher Wetterdienst (DWD), the warmest night ever recorded in Germany.

Over that same week, all-cause mortality ran 32 percent above the four-year average for the same period, with approximately 23,900 deaths recorded nationwide in a single week.

## Vulnerable populations already overwhelmed

In an early-August snapshot covering 11,900 deaths, more than 6,000 involved people aged 85 and older, with roughly 2,900 more among those aged 75 to 84. Most German nursing homes remain without air conditioning to this day, a structural gap that several public health observers point to as a direct driver of the excess mortality among the oldest residents.

At this pace, Germany is approaching the all-time European national record set by Italy during the 2022 heat season.

## What this toll means for frontline responders

A mortality count of this scale is not only a public health statistic. Week after week, it translated into real pressure on emergency services: fire crews confronting a vegetation fire danger index at its maximum level, and emergency medical teams whose call volume jumped by double-digit percentages during the late-June peak. Both of these threads, operational for Germany's Feuerwehr and clinical for its Rettungsdienst, deserve dedicated coverage, since the same risk factors driving mortality among the public are also bearing down on the responders themselves.
