Sizing a baptism pool correctly is the first technical question a church faces when equipping itself for the first time. A pool that's too small makes the moment awkward; one that's oversized complicates filling and transport.

## Reference dimensions for an adult

To allow **full immersion** of an adult lying down, with room for the officiant to assist from either side of the pool, these dimensions are a solid baseline:

- **Length**: at least 79 in (200 cm), enough for a person lying down without restriction
- **Width**: 28 to 31 in (70 to 80 cm), enough room for the officiant's arms
- **Water depth**: 20 to 24 in (50 to 60 cm), enough to fully cover a candidate lying down

The Kollder portable baptistry follows exactly these proportions, at 205 × 80 × 60 cm (81 × 31 × 24 in) set up.

## How much water to plan for

For a pool of these dimensions, plan for **roughly 130 to 185 gallons of water** depending on the fill level chosen. That volume can be reached with a standard garden hose in 15 to 30 minutes depending on flow rate, or with water containers where no hose access is nearby.

Unlike a medical cooling application, the water doesn't need to be cold: room temperature, or lightly warmed with hot water added to the mix, gives candidates a comfortable experience.

## What about baptizing a child or teenager?

The same dimensions work, with the fill level simply adjusted to the candidate's size. A pool sized for an adult stays versatile for every age practicing immersion, with no need for a second piece of equipment.
