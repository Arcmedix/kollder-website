France's fire and rescue services have rarely been this angry. A month-long strike notice, around 8,000 firefighters marching through Paris, and an open revolt from the departments that fund them: the summer of 2026 turned a long-running funding debate into a national political issue. Here is what happened, what the SDIS (departmental fire and rescue services) are demanding, and why firefighter health sits at the center of it.

## How the French fire service is funded

France has roughly one hundred SDIS, one per department. Their combined spending reached about 6 billion euros in 2024, 54% funded by the departments and 35% by municipalities and intercommunal bodies. The departments' share is partly financed by a slice of the insurance contracts tax (TSCA), worth 1.45 billion euros in 2024.

The workforce relies heavily on volunteers: about 200,000 volunteer firefighters make up roughly 80% of personnel. Activity keeps rising, with **4.89 million interventions in 2025**, most of them medical calls. A 2024 National Assembly fact-finding mission put the SDIS' cumulative debt at **1.4 billion euros**.

## The summer that broke the system

On July 22, 2026, a fire starting near Saumos in Gironde became the most destructive forest fire in France in more than fifty years. Together with fires in the Landes, 47,004 hectares burned in the southwest according to State services, 88 firefighters were injured and thousands of firefighters were deployed alongside 1,500 military personnel. Major fires followed in the Aude and elsewhere.

Nationwide, close to 100,000 hectares burned. Four firefighters died in the line of duty over the summer.

## Timeline of the mobilization

- **September 8, 2026**: eight of the nine firefighter unions file a one-month strike notice demanding more staff and equipment.
- **September 9**: Départements de France, the association of French departments, publicly voices "disappointment" and "anger" at the government's civil security modernization bill, arguing the funding model is exhausted.
- **September 27 to 29**: firefighters converge on Paris, and around 8,000 demonstrate on the 29th behind the banner "Des moyens, pas des miracles" (resources, not miracles).
- **October 2**: in a video address to the national firefighters' congress, Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez promises **an extra one billion euros over five years**.

## What firefighters are demanding

- **Sustainable funding**, not one-off top-ups, most likely through a larger share of the insurance tax.
- **More professional firefighters**: the CGT and SUD unions call for 21,000 additional career firefighters.
- **Pay**: revaluation of the civil service pay index.
- **Equipment and health**: engines, aerial resources, protective equipment and real protection of personnel health after a summer of extreme heat exposure.

## What the government offered

The package relies on an exceptional increase in the insurance tax written into the **2027 finance bill**: **200 million euros of extra revenue per year** for the SDIS, or one billion over five years. The minister also pledged to double the number of leased helicopters and light aircraft for the 2027 fire season.

Unions remain cautious. The FA/SPP-PATS union said an announcement is not a lasting budget commitment and that firefighters will judge by the final texts and the money actually received. Spread evenly across about one hundred services, 200 million euros would average roughly 2 million euros per SDIS per year, so every allocation will be contested.

## Firefighter health and heat: the budget line that matters

Among all the demands, protecting firefighters' health is the one every union shares. The 2026 season showed crews working multi-day wildfire deployments in full protective gear under intense heat. Exertional heat stroke is a life-threatening emergency, but survival is very high when the victim is cooled immediately on site.

The standard is **"Cool First, Transport Second"** (ACSM 2023, IOC / BJSM 2021, Korey Stringer Institute). Whole-body cold water immersion cools at up to **0.35 °C per minute** (Casa et al. 2007), while ice packs, ice sheets, fans or misting reach roughly 0.03 to 0.08 °C per minute (Filep et al., Medicina 2020). Only immersion reliably exceeds the 0.15 °C per minute threshold the literature considers adequate.
