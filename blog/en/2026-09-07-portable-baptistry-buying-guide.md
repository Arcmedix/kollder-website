Before placing an order, a few technical checks can save a church from a costly mistake. Here's what to verify systematically when comparing portable baptistries.

## Structure type

Check whether the baptistry is **inflatable** (soft PVC structure, set up by pumping) or a **rigid folding** design (metal frame, set up by unfolding). The latter offers constant stability and a longer lifespan, though its folded weight is usually higher. The right choice mostly depends on expected frequency of use: occasional services versus regular baptisms year-round.

## Actual dimensions

Confirm that the stated dimensions allow for **full immersion** of an adult lying down, not just a seated or partially standing position. A length of at least 79 in (200 cm) and 20 to 24 in (50 to 60 cm) of water depth is a reliable baseline.

## The material in contact with water

For equipment reused several times a year, the material matters as much as the structure. **Food-grade stainless steel** cleans and disinfects easily, with no degradation over time, unlike PVC, which loses its seal over repeated folding and unfolding cycles.

## Setup time and ease

Ask for the actual setup time, by one person, with no tools. This is a criterion that often gets glossed over in product listings, but it matters on the day of the service: a setup that pulls in several volunteers for 20 minutes is a very different experience from a solo setup in under 2 minutes.

## Folded weight and size

Check the folded weight and dimensions, especially if the baptistry needs to travel regularly (multi-site churches, camps, conventions) or be stored in a limited space.

## Available accessories

Entry rail, steps, wheeled carry bag, drain system: these details simplify everyday use. Check what's included in the base price and what stays optional.
